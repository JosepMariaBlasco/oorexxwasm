Grokking in Rexx

A small neural network learns addition modulo 13 from 153 examples. It memorises them in a few dozen epochs, and only hundreds of epochs later does it get right the 16 sums it has never seen: that late jump is called grokking. The network, its training and the drawings are all one ooRexx program, printed here, running in your browser.

What you are looking at

Loss and accuracy. Train is how the network does on the 153 pairs it learns from; test, on the 16 it never sees. Train reaches 100% almost at once: that is memorising. Test stays low for a long time, then jumps. The red line marks the epoch when every test pair is right.

The table. The 169 sums a + b mod 13, as the network answers them now: green is right, red is wrong. The framed cells are the test pairs: they turn green long after all the others.

Fourier energy. Each number 0–12 enters the network as a list of 12 weights, its embedding. Read along the 13 numbers, those weights can be split into waves of frequency k. The darker a row, the more of the embeddings is in that wave; the bars show the same thing now.

Why it matters. A network that has grokked has stopped storing a list of answers: it places the numbers on circles, like the hours of a clock, and adds by turning. The few dark rows are those circles.
